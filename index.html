Structural virology · Utrecht University

Molecular machines of positive-strand RNA viruses

My group investigates the molecular machines that enable positive-strand RNA viruses to move their genomes across biological barriers. We study viruses of medical and veterinary importance, with the aim of uncovering fundamental principles of viral entry, replication and assembly, and translating these insights into new antiviral strategies.

Research themes

Three interconnected themes

01

Molecular machines controlling virus entry

We investigate how viral attachment proteins recognise cellular receptors and how receptor engagement triggers structural rearrangements that enable entry. A particular interest is understanding how glycan and protein receptors regulate the conformational dynamics of coronavirus spike proteins.

02

Molecular machines controlling viral RNA replication and transport

We study the poorly understood AAA+ ATPases encoded by positive-strand RNA viruses. We aim to determine how these proteins assemble, interact with membranes and RNA, and use ATP hydrolysis to coordinate genome replication, transport and assembly.

03

Targeting viral molecular machines with antivirals

Because these proteins perform essential mechanical steps in the viral life cycle, they also present attractive therapeutic targets. We use mechanistic and structural insights to identify vulnerabilities that can be exploited by small molecules, peptides, antibodies and other biologics.

The team

Part of the Utrecht University Virology Section

Our research group is embedded within the Utrecht University Virology Section at the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine. Together, we aim to understand viruses in detail, from their molecular biology to their ability to cross species barriers, and to translate that knowledge into vaccines, antiviral therapies, and diagnostic tools.
